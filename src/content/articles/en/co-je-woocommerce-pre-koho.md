WooCommerce is the most common answer to "what should I build an online store on if I already have, or want, WordPress?". It is not the right answer for everyone, though. Here is, without marketing, what it is, what it gives you and when another route is better. I am writing this as someone who builds and maintains e-shops on it.

## What WooCommerce is

WooCommerce is a **WordPress plugin**. WordPress handles content (pages, articles, media) and WooCommerce adds everything a store needs: products and variations, cart and checkout, orders, customers, taxes, coupons and shipping. It is open source, so the code is open and your store is not locked into one vendor.

The key difference from hosted platforms: **the store is yours**. You (or your developer) decide on hosting, design, data and what the store can do.

## What it gives you

- **Freedom in design and features.** You can shape the theme and behaviour to your needs instead of the platform's limits.
- **Content and sales in one place.** Blog, case studies, category pages with intro text and products live in one system, which also helps SEO.
- **Local ecosystem.** Payment gateways (for example GoPay, Comgate, Stripe), shipping services and invoicing systems have integrations. Always check the current support of the specific extension.
- **No per-order fee from the platform.** You pay the payment gateway's fees, not a commission to the store software.
- **Easier to change later.** The data stays yours, so changing the design or hosting is not a migration out of someone else's system.

## What is not free

This is where "the plugin is free" and "the store is free" part ways:

- **Hosting.** A store needs more than the cheapest plan. A slow server shows up straight away in page speed and at checkout. See [Slovak hosting performance compared](/en/blog/hostingy-sk-vykon/).
- **Maintenance.** WordPress, WooCommerce and plugins get regular updates. Someone has to apply and test them and keep backups.
- **Extensions.** The core is broad, but specifics (shipping carriers, B2B pricing, advanced filters) usually mean another plugin or custom code.
- **Responsibility for security and performance.** On a hosted platform the provider handles it. Here it is on you.

## Who it suits

WooCommerce makes sense if at least one of these is true:

1. **You already have a WordPress website** and want to add selling without starting a new system.
2. **You want control and independence** from a single vendor, even at the price of more responsibility.
3. **You need something custom:** your own filters, special pricing logic, an integration with an internal system, B2B selling.
4. **Content is part of selling** (guides, blog, comparisons) and you want traffic from it.
5. **You expect long-term growth** and don't want to move off a limiting platform in a few years.

For a store with a larger catalogue it is often the sensible route, provided it is built properly. ## Who should look elsewhere

- **You don't want to deal with the technical side at all** and have nobody to manage it. For simple selling a hosted platform (for example Shoptet or Shopify) may be better, since the provider handles upkeep.
- **You want to launch in a few days** with a ready-made template and configure nothing.
- **You have few products and a minimal maintenance budget.** Then also read the [WooCommerce vs Shopify comparison](/en/blog/woocommerce-vs-shopify/) to see the difference in cost and flexibility.

There is no "best" platform. There is the one that fits your scale, budget and willingness to look after it (or pay someone who will).

## What to watch out for with WooCommerce

- **Number of plugins.** Each extra one is a potential slowdown and a risk during updates. Fewer, proven ones are better.
- **Filter and search performance** in larger catalogues. Standard solutions can slow down with thousands of products, which is why larger stores often use a custom or tuned filter.
- **Orders and database.** Newer WooCommerce versions store orders more efficiently (HPOS); older stores need a migration for it: [WooCommerce HPOS migration](/en/blog/woocommerce-hpos-migracia/).
- **Payments and shipping.** Before launch, test a real order flow, not only the look. Gateway overview: [Payment gateways in Slovakia](/en/blog/sk-platobne-brany-2026/).
