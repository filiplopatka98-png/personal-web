A company website can look good and still stay silent. The phone doesn't ring, the form sends nothing, and the site is just a business card a few friends look at. The difference between a business card and a site that wins work isn't the effects - it's how quickly a visitor understands what you do and how easily they can get in touch.

Take the construction company [Profi House](/en/work/profihouse/): in the six months after launch the site had more than 2,500 visits and over 60 enquiries. Roughly one in forty visitors got in touch. Here are the five things behind it.

## 1. The first screen: what you do and for whom

Visitors decide within seconds whether they're in the right place. The first screen - without scrolling - has to make clear what you do, for whom and where.

On the Profi House site, the types of construction the company does are right at the top. Anyone looking to build a house or renovate sees it immediately and doesn't have to dig through the menu.

What doesn't belong there: generic lines like "your reliable partner" and a five-photo slider where people only ever see the first one.

## 2. Form and phone always within reach

Once a visitor decides, they shouldn't have to search for how to contact you. A form or contact button should be available from every page, on mobile too.

- A short form: name, contact, message. Every extra field puts some people off.
- A specific call to action: "Request a free consultation" works better than "Contact us".
- The phone number as a clickable link - one tap on mobile.
- Test your own form. A broken form is the most expensive mistake, because you don't know about it.

## 3. Projects by category

People want to see whether you've done something like their job before. A gallery of everything at once doesn't help.

Profi House splits its portfolio into categories - family houses, restaurants, garages - with before-and-after photos. Customers quickly find their type of job and see the result, not just a finished facade.

## 4. Mobile speed and local SEO

Most people look for a company on their phone. If the main content takes long to load, some of them leave before they see it. The Profi House site runs on a custom WordPress theme without a heavy page builder, and the main content (LCP) loads in under 1.5 seconds - galleries load only on scroll and images are served as WebP. If your site is slow, causes and fixes are in the article on [LCP over 2.5 seconds](/en/blog/lcp-nad-2-5s-pricin/).

The other half is being found at all. For a company working in a region, that means local SEO: services and towns in the copy, separate pages for the main services and a complete Google Business Profile.

## 5. Tracking: where enquiries come from

Without tracking you don't know whether the site works or what to improve. You don't need much:

- track form submissions and phone clicks as conversions (for example in Google Analytics),
- ask "How did you hear about us?" at first contact,
- once a month, check how many enquiries came in and from which source.

Only with these numbers can you tell whether paid ads are worth it, whether the copy needs work, or whether people leave on a specific page.

## Summary

A website that brings in enquiries has a clear first screen, contact always within reach, projects by category, is fast on mobile and tracks where people come from. None of this is expensive - it's mostly about thinking of it from the start when the site is built.
