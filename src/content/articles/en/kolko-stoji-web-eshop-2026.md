As a rough guide: a company website starts at €390, a WooCommerce store at €1,490, and speeding up an existing site at €390. You know the exact price upfront — after a short conversation about what the site actually needs to do.

## What drives the price (not page count)

**Scope and features.** A simple company site costs less than one with bookings, multiple languages or a customer area. A store with variants, coupons and shipping rules is a different league from a small catalogue.

**Custom theme vs. page builder.** I build on a custom WordPress theme without heavy page builders. The result is faster, original and ranks better on Google — and you still manage content yourself.

**Integrations.** Payment gateways, inventory or accounting sync (Pohoda, Money S3), CRM, email marketing — each one is extra work, testing and maintenance.

## Why an online store costs more than a company website

The jump from €390 to €1,490 is a jump in complexity. A store has a whole transaction flow that must work flawlessly, because your customers' money runs through it: payments, stock levels and a checkout that doesn't scare people away — more on that in a [separate article](/en/blog/checkout-konvertuje-9-uprav/).

## Concrete numbers

- **Company website — from €390, 2–4 weeks.** Custom WordPress theme, responsive design, SEO basics, contact form, domain and hosting for the first year, training and 3 months of support.
- **WooCommerce store — from €1,490, 4–8 weeks.** Payment gateway, shipping, invoicing integration (optional inventory), order tracking and 6 months of support.
- **Website & store speed-up — from €390, 1–2 weeks.** Speed audit and Core Web Vitals fixes for a fixed price. For example, natur-life.sk went from a mobile score of 27 to 83.
- **Maintenance — from €39/month** (stores from €79/month). Updates, backups and uptime monitoring; extra changes €30/h.
- **Website check — free.** Within 2 business days: mobile speed, SEO and the 3 most important fixes. In-depth consulting €30/h.

These are starting prices — a specific project can land higher depending on scope. Pricing models (hourly vs. fixed vs. hybrid) are covered in the article on [pricing for small online stores](/en/blog/cenotvorba-eshop-models/).

## Why a "€150 website" often ends up costing more

Cheap offers sometimes make sense, but usually mean a template you can't edit, no speed optimization, no support after handover, or skipped security and SEO basics you pay for later. A good test: ask the vendor directly what the price doesn't include.
